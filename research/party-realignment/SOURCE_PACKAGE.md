## Primary-source turning points

### 1856 Republican platform

Use to establish the new party's opposition to giving slavery legal existence in the territories.

Do **not** describe the platform as an immediate nationwide abolition platform. The territorial-expansion distinction matters.

### 1860 Republican platform

Useful because it shows two things at once:

1. opposition to slavery's extension;
2. continuity with national-development policies such as tariffs and a Pacific railroad.

This directly undermines a simple “old Republicans = modern Democrats” mapping.

### 1860 Democratic split

The regular Democratic platform acknowledges intraparty disagreement over slavery in the territories.

The Breckinridge/Southern Democratic platform goes further and demands federal protection of property rights in the territories and Fugitive Slave Law enforcement.

Use faction labels carefully.

### 1948 Democratic vs. States' Rights Democratic platforms

This is the clearest primary-source snapshot of the Democratic civil-rights contradiction becoming politically unsustainable.

The national Democratic platform embraces civil rights.

The Dixiecrat platform explicitly defends segregation.

### 1960 Democratic and Republican platforms

Both national parties make substantial civil-rights commitments.

Use this to show why the transition cannot be described as already complete by 1960.

### 1964 Democratic and Republican platforms

Both support the newly enacted Civil Rights Act.

This is important counterevidence to any simplistic claim that the GOP became an anti-civil-rights party in one stroke in 1964.

At the same time, the election map and Goldwater's victory in five Deep South states show that major regional movement was underway.

## Specialist historiography

### Kuziemko & Washington (2018)

Peer-reviewed empirical study in the *American Economic Review*.

Important finding:

- in their data, racially conservative Southern White defection explains the full decline in relative Southern White Democratic identification from 1958–1980 and most of the decline through 2000.

Use as strong evidence that race/civil rights was central.

Do **not** convert the paper into:

> race explains every dimension of Southern or national partisan change.

Its question and data are narrower.

### Kousser (2010)

Annual Review synthesis.

Useful counterweight to overly simple monocausal narratives: the categories of race, class, religion, ideology, party, and governance themselves changed and interacted.

Use to justify a layered conclusion without falsely treating all causal explanations as equally supported.

## Important interpretive boundaries

### “The parties switched”

Allowed only as explicitly qualified shorthand.

Preferred:

> party coalitions realigned and ideologically sorted over generations.

### “Whigs became Democrats”

Do not use.

Institutional lineage is primarily:

```text
many former Whigs
+ Free Soilers
+ anti-slavery / Northern Democrats
→ Republican Party
```

The modern Democratic Party later came to hold some positions that resemble parts of Whig national-development thinking. That is **ideological recombination**, not party descent.

### “Republicans freed the slaves”

Historically accurate as a compressed description of Republican leadership in emancipation/abolition.

Do not use it to infer modern ideological continuity without additional evidence.

### “Democrats were segregationists”

Historically accurate for the Southern Democratic/Jim Crow power structure when stated with period and faction.

Do not use it to erase the Northern liberal Democratic wing or the later realignment.

## Remaining publication risks

1. The entry spans nearly two centuries and therefore necessarily compresses many intermediate developments.
2. The exact relative weight of race, economics, religion, and culture varies by period and outcome.
3. “Liberal” and “conservative” themselves change meaning over time.
4. Congressional ideology measures are not interchangeable with voter ideology.
5. State/local Southern realignment lagged presidential realignment and varied by state.

These are reasons for careful wording, not blockers to the section's central model.
