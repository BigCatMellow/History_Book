# Independent Review Request — How America's Political Parties Changed

- Method: [Book Build System](../../docs/BOOK_BUILD_SYSTEM.md)

The authoring pass must not approve its own high-risk political-history synthesis.

## Review question

Does the entry accurately explain American party change without:

- implying a literal party swap;
- minimizing Republican leadership in abolition/Reconstruction/civil-rights legislation;
- erasing the Southern Democratic segregationist tradition;
- treating the 1960s as the beginning of all realignment;
- understating the central role of race/civil rights in Southern White partisan change;
- reducing the entire long realignment to race alone;
- projecting modern left/right categories backward without qualification?

## Required checks

### 1. Party lineage

Verify that:

- Jacksonian Democrats → Democratic Party continuity is described accurately;
- Whigs do **not** become modern Democrats;
- former Whigs + Free Soilers + anti-slavery/Northern Democrats are represented accurately in Republican formation.

### 2. Slavery and Reconstruction

Verify that:

- early Republican opposition is accurately described as centered initially on **slavery's expansion**;
- abolition/Reconstruction Republican leadership is stated clearly;
- faction/party wording does not overstate unanimity.

### 3. New Deal realignment

Verify that:

- Black voter movement is dated before the 1960s;
- New Deal benefits and discriminatory limitations are both represented;
- Southern Democratic power inside the New Deal coalition is not hidden.

### 4. Civil-rights transition

Verify primary-source treatment of:

- 1948 Democratic platform;
- 1948 States' Rights Democratic/Dixiecrat platform;
- 1960 Democratic and Republican platforms;
- 1964 Democratic and Republican platforms;
- Senate Civil Rights Act history.

Check that the entry shows genuine bipartisan civil-rights support before sorting was complete.

### 5. Southern realignment causation

Check that:

- House history supports the “decades-long” description;
- Kuziemko/Washington is represented within the scope of its actual result;
- Kousser is not used to create false equivalence between causal explanations;
- the final synthesis appropriately distinguishes “race was central” from “race explains everything.”

### 6. Ideological sorting

Verify that Voteview evidence is used specifically for congressional ideology/overlap and is not silently generalized to every voter.

### 7. Teaching

A novice should be able to explain the difference among:

```text
party organization
coalition
faction
ideology
```

The transfer exercise should test that model rather than merely ask for recall.
